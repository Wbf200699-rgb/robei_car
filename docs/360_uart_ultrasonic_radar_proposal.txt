360 Degree UART Ultrasonic Radar Proposal
=========================================

Project context
---------------
The car already has a low-mounted forward ultrasonic sensor. It is suitable for
wheel-height collision protection and chassis safety. A future 360 degree UART
ultrasonic radar can add surrounding-space awareness, radar visualization, and a
stronger demo story.

Recommended project story
-------------------------
The car has two sensing layers:

1. Low ultrasonic sensor
   - Watches the wheel-height area.
   - Handles close-range front collision and chassis safety.
   - Has the highest safety priority.

2. 360 degree UART ultrasonic radar
   - Watches the surrounding environment.
   - Builds a local point cloud around the car.
   - Provides sector danger levels and open-space awareness.

The FPGA acts as the real-time safety brain. It receives sensor data, updates the
display, judges danger, and stops the car with deterministic low latency. The
Bluetooth app acts as the interaction and visualization layer.

Functions suitable for FPGA
---------------------------
These are realistic and useful to implement in Verilog:

1. UART radar receiver
   - Receive radar frames from the 360 degree ultrasonic radar.
   - Parse angle and distance fields.
   - Validate frames with header/checksum if the radar protocol provides one.

2. Radar point cache
   - Store distance by angle.
   - Options:
     - 360 bins, one distance per degree.
     - 180 bins, one distance per two degrees.
     - 72 bins, one distance per five degrees for simpler display.

3. HDMI radar display
   - Draw the car at the center.
   - Draw surrounding radar points as a 2D ring/point cloud.
   - Use color by distance:
     - Green: safe/far.
     - Yellow: caution.
     - Red: danger/near.

4. Safety bubble
   - Define a safety radius around the car, for example 30 cm.
   - Any radar point inside the safety bubble triggers warning or stop.
   - Low-mounted ultrasonic stop has priority over radar-based warning.

5. Danger sectors
   - Split 360 degrees into 8 or 12 sectors.
   - Track the minimum distance in each sector.
   - Display sector danger on HDMI.
   - Send sector summary to the Bluetooth app.

6. Emergency stop / motion inhibit
   - If the front sector or low ultrasonic sensor reports danger, stop forward
     motion.
   - Optionally inhibit turning toward a dangerous side sector.
   - Keep this logic simple and deterministic in the first version.

7. Bluetooth summary output
   - Send compact radar/status frames to the app.
   - Prefer sector minimum distances first; full point cloud can be added later.

Functions better for the app or future extension
------------------------------------------------
These are possible, but not ideal as pure FPGA first-version work:

1. SLAM or long-term global mapping.
2. Complex path replanning.
3. Automatic parking space search.
4. Advanced obstacle avoidance strategy.
5. Beautiful historical visualization and logs.

The app is a better place for rich visualization, history, parameter tuning, and
more complex decision explanations.

Suggested implementation stages
-------------------------------
Stage 1: Bring-up and display
  - Add UART RX driver for the radar.
  - Parse angle and distance.
  - Cache points by angle.
  - Draw 360 radar points on HDMI.
  - Add a simple Bluetooth radar summary frame.

Stage 2: Safety bubble
  - Compute 8 or 12 sector minimum distances.
  - Show sector danger colors on HDMI.
  - Combine low ultrasonic and radar danger signals.
  - Stop or inhibit motion when danger is detected.

Stage 3: App visualization
  - App connects through CH9141K BLE-UART.
  - App keeps sending P heartbeats once per second.
  - App receives status, ACK, and radar summary frames.
  - App draws a nicer 360 radar view and shows danger sectors.

Stage 4: Smarter behavior
  - When blocked, stop first.
  - Compare left/right/back sector clearance.
  - Suggest or execute a simple safe turn direction.
  - Keep route replanning optional.

Possible Bluetooth frame design
-------------------------------
Existing frames:
  @PONG\n
  @K<KEY>\n
  @S...\n

Possible new radar summary frames:
  @Z<sector_count><d0><d1>...<dn>\n

Example:
  @Z8 0030 0048 0120 0200 0180 0060 0040 0028\n

Where:
  - Z means radar zone/sector summary.
  - sector_count is 8 or 12.
  - each distance is a sector minimum distance in cm.

Possible point frame for debug:
  @R<angle_deg>,<distance_cm>\n

Example:
  @R090,045\n

The first version should prefer @Z sector summary because it is compact and easy
for FPGA and app parsing.

Demo-friendly explanation
-------------------------
The low ultrasonic sensor is the car's close-range bumper. The 360 degree radar
is the car's surrounding awareness. Together they form a local safety bubble.
The FPGA is responsible for real-time sensing, display, and emergency decisions,
while the phone app provides human-friendly visualization and control.

Recommended first deliverable
-----------------------------
Implement:
  - UART radar receive and parser.
  - Angle-distance cache.
  - HDMI 360 radar display.
  - 8-sector danger summary.
  - Safety bubble stop signal.
  - Bluetooth @Z sector summary frame.

This is feasible on the FPGA, easy to demo, and leaves advanced mapping or path
planning for later.
